Workspace access

Who can see and change forms and responses in QCMS, and how to give, change and take away that access.

This guide describes how workspace access is designed to work. Parts of it are not yet available in the product.

How access works

  • Your account is your sign-in for the whole site. On its own, an account lets you do nothing except manage itself.
  • A workspace is the only boundary. Forms and questions belong to one workspace. There is no separate access per form: if you can do something to one form in a workspace, you can do it to every form there. A site that needs separate zones uses separate workspaces.
  • An access group belongs to one workspace. It has a set of roles (what you can do) and a set of environments (where, such as test or prod). Everyone in the group holds every role in it, in every environment it names, for every form in the workspace.
  • Groups add up. If you are in two groups, you can do everything either one allows.
  • Environments are named one by one. There is no "all environments" choice. View forms and Edit forms work the same everywhere, so they need no environment. Publish forms, View responses and Erase responses work only in the environments the group names.
  • Administrators administer. A Workspace administrator manages one workspace's people, groups, settings and audit. A Site administrator manages accounts, workspaces and the site. Neither role lets you see forms or responses. To do that, an administrator is added to an access group like anyone else.
  • New forms need no setup. When a form is created, the workspace's groups apply to it straight away. Creating, publishing or closing a form never changes any group.
  • The assistant follows your access. It can only show or do what you could do yourself on the screen.
  • Changes apply at once. When access is granted or revoked, or an account is disabled, the person's next action is checked against the new access.

Starter groups

Every new workspace starts with four access groups. They are ordinary groups: you can rename, change or delete them.

The four starter groups
Group Roles Environments Typical member
Authors Edit forms, Publish forms, View responses test Builds forms and tries them out in test
Publishers Publish forms prod Puts tested versions live and runs them
Response viewers View responses prod Reads and exports real responses
Response erasers Erase responses prod Handles erasure requests
  • Starter groups start empty, except on a one-person site.
  • Someone who builds in test and also publishes to prod belongs to two groups, Authors and Publishers. That keeps production access visible.
  • Responses in test are still personal data. Authors can read them because the group says so, not by default.
  • On a site with more environments than test and prod, such as dev, Authors covers every environment except prod. Publishers, Response viewers and Response erasers cover prod.
  • On a site whose only environment is prod, Authors has Edit forms only. Publishing and reading responses there come from Publishers and Response viewers.
  • Starter groups are a starting point, not protected. A Workspace administrator can rename or delete them.

For everyone

See your access

Who: everyone · Where: Account menu, My access

  1. Open the Account menu in the top bar.
  2. Choose My access.

You see each action, by environment, with the access group that grants it, or "no group grants this".

Find out why you can't do something

Who: everyone

  • Where an action is unavailable on a Forms, Operations or Responses screen, QCMS shows it disabled with a short reason.
  • Check My access. If no group grants the action in that environment, ask a Workspace administrator of that workspace to add you to a group that does.
  • If you have no access to anything yet, your home page says so, names an administrator to ask, and links to My access.
  • You only see the workspaces you hold something in.

Manage your account

Who: everyone, administrators included · Where: Account menu, My account

Manage your own password, second factor, recovery codes and sessions. Nobody else does this for you. If you are locked out, ask a Site administrator to reset your sign-in.

Sign in for the first time

Who: a person whose account a Site administrator has just created

  1. Open the sign-in email QCMS sent to your address and follow its link. The link works once and lasts 72 hours.
  2. Set your password.
  3. Enrol a second factor. You cannot do anything else until both are done.

Nobody else sees your link or chooses your password. After you sign in, you see whatever access a Workspace administrator has given you, or the no-access home page.

If refused: a link that has been used or has expired does not work. Ask a Site administrator to send a new one.

For Workspace administrators

You manage one workspace from its Workspace area: Members, Access groups, Settings, Audit and Archive. A Site administrator can do all of this in every workspace too. Being an administrator does not let you see forms or responses; add yourself to a group if you need that.

Add a person to the workspace

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Members or Access groups

  1. Enter the person's exact email address. You cannot browse or search the site's accounts.
  2. Choose the access group or groups they need (see What each role lets you do).
  3. Check the preview: it shows what they gain, in which environments, and whether approval is needed.
  4. Confirm with Grant.

The person can use the new access on their next action. The grant is recorded in the workspace audit.

If refused: an address that is unknown, disabled, or belongs to an account not yet claimed gets the same general answer for all three. Ask a Site administrator to check or create the account.

Adding yourself to a group that has View responses or Erase responses needs an approver while second approval is on. So does changing a group you are in so that it gains one of those roles, or a new environment for one. With it off, you can do it yourself, and the grant and every later read are recorded in the audit.

Change or remove someone's access

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Members (the person's access) or Access groups

  1. Open the person, or the access group.
  2. Grant them another group, or Revoke them from one.
  3. Check the preview of what they gain or lose, then confirm.

Revoke always works and takes effect on the person's next action. The only exception is the last Workspace administrator, below.

Appoint or remove a Workspace administrator

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Members

Workspace administrator is set on the person, not through a group. A workspace must keep at least one active Workspace administrator who has claimed their account. For the last one, Remove or Demote is disabled for everyone, Site administrators included, with the reason and a link to appoint someone else first. Being a Site administrator does not count as being that workspace's administrator.

Create or change an access group

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Access groups

  1. Open Access groups, then New access group, or open an existing group.
  2. Name it and tick the roles it carries: View forms, Edit forms, Publish forms, View responses, Erase responses.
  3. If you ticked Publish forms, View responses or Erase responses, tick each environment it should cover. Such a group must cover at least one environment. A group with only View forms or Edit forms shows no environment choice.
  4. Check the preview of who gains or loses what, then confirm.

The group applies to every form in the workspace at once. Saving never adds an environment you did not tick. A group never reaches into another workspace.

If you are in the group yourself and second approval is on, adding View responses or Erase responses to it, or a new environment for those roles, waits for an approver. The approver approves the group as it was when you asked.

You choose each group's name, and you can rename or delete any group, starter groups included. If no group covers a role or an environment any more, Access groups shows a notice.

When a new environment appears

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Access groups

A new environment is covered by no group, so nobody can publish, view responses or erase responses there yet. Access groups shows a notice such as "Environment X: no group covers it yet." Open each group that should cover it, tick the environment, and confirm.

Turn second approval on or off

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Settings

Second approval is off by default and applies only in prod. While it is on:

  • The person who froze a version cannot publish it to prod. Someone else with Publish forms for prod does, and is recorded as the approver.
  • A webhook change in prod is held until someone else with Publish forms for prod confirms it.
  • On a site whose only environment is prod, publishing a draft to prod becomes a request. Someone else with Publish forms for prod approves it on the form, and only then is the draft frozen as the next version and made live.
  • Every form move out of this workspace needs an approver who is not the mover: a Workspace administrator of this workspace or a Site administrator. This applies to Workspace administrators and Site administrators too.
  • Links and opening or closing a form need no approval.
  • Turning the rule off, and giving yourself a response role or a new environment for one, need an approver: a different Workspace administrator of this workspace, or a Site administrator who is not you. A Site administrator who is not you can always approve.
  • Nobody can approve their own request.
  • When a request is approved, QCMS checks again that the person who asked can still do what they asked. If they no longer can, for example because they lost Edit forms in a workspace a form is moving to, the approval is refused.

If second approval is on and nobody else could approve any more, for example because the other administrator was disabled, Settings shows a warning.

A request to turn the rule off, or to give yourself response access, waits for the approver. Workspace administrators find it under Requests on the workspace's Audit screen; Site administrators find it under Approval requests in Site configuration. A request lasts 7 days.

If refused: turning it on is refused when nobody else could approve, because there is no second administrator (another Workspace administrator, or a Site administrator) or no second person with Publish forms for prod.

Re-confirm a suspended membership

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Members

After a Site administrator resets someone's sign-in or reactivates their account, that person's places in groups with View responses or Erase responses are suspended in your workspace. Their other access works as normal. Re-confirm each suspended membership you still want. A Site administrator can re-confirm too. By default, where there is an administrator other than both the person who did the reset or reactivation and the person affected, the re-confirmation must come from such an administrator. Where there is none, the person who did the reset or reactivation may re-confirm, and the audit records it.

Nobody re-confirms their own membership, whatever their role. Being a Workspace administrator is not suspended by a reset or reactivation.

Rename the workspace

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Settings

Change the name. The workspace's identifier in web addresses stays the same.

Read the workspace audit

Who: Workspace administrator of this workspace, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Audit

The audit records response reads, exports and erasures; every access change (groups, members, administrators, re-confirmations); workspace changes (rename, archive, forms moved in or out, the second approval switch); assistant actions; and site-level acts that affect this workspace, such as a reset of one of its members. Nobody can edit or delete an entry.

Archive the workspace

Who: Workspace administrator, Site administrator · Where: Workspace area, Archive (shown when the site has two or more workspaces)

  1. Move every form out of the workspace first, including closed and never-published ones.
  2. Open Archive and type the workspace name to confirm.

Archiving cannot be undone.

If refused: archive is refused while the workspace owns any form, or while it is the shared question library. The reason is shown on the screen.

Move a form to another workspace

Who: anyone with Edit forms in both workspaces, with an approver when the move is held · Where: Forms, the form's Move form screen

  1. Open the form and choose Move form.
  2. Pick the destination workspace.
  3. Read the confirmation: it names who gains and who loses access, because the destination's groups will apply.
  4. Confirm.

With second approval off in the workspace the form is leaving, a move by one of its Workspace administrators happens at once. Anyone else's move waits until one of them, or a Site administrator, approves it. With second approval on, every move waits for an approver other than you, even if you are a Workspace administrator or a Site administrator. A Site administrator can approve someone else's move, never their own. The form's responses move with it. No group changes in either workspace.

If refused: you need Edit forms in the destination workspace too. Ask its Workspace administrator.

Questions cannot be moved between workspaces. To reuse a question across workspaces, put it in the shared question library.

The shared question library

A site can mark one workspace as the shared question library; a Site administrator does this in Site configuration. Everyone with Edit forms in any workspace can see and pin its questions, which show a shared tag. Only people with Edit forms in the library workspace itself can change them. Publishing a question freezes a version others can pin; it involves no environment.

Before a workspace is marked, the confirmation states who will be able to see its questions. Marking and unmarking are recorded in the site audit and in that workspace's audit.

When someone leaves

  • In your workspace, Revoke the person from each group.
  • If they are a Workspace administrator, appoint someone else before removing them.
  • Ask a Site administrator to disable their account. Only a Site administrator can do that, and it ends their access in every workspace at once.

For Site administrators

You work from Site configuration: Accounts, Site administrators, Approval requests, Site audit, Workspaces, New workspace and Shared question library. You can also administer every workspace as its Workspace administrator would. Being a Site administrator does not let you read forms or responses; add yourself to a group in a workspace if you need that.

A Site administrator is trusted at site level. A Site administrator could, for example, create a second account and use it to approve their own request. The audit is the check on what Site administrators do.

Create an account

Where: Site configuration, Accounts

  1. Enter the person's email address.
  2. Confirm. QCMS emails a single-use sign-in link, valid for 72 hours, straight to that address.

If refused: you cannot create accounts until email is set up for the site. Only the very first account, created when QCMS is installed, is made without it.

You never see the link or a password. Until the person uses the link, the account is unclaimed: it cannot act, cannot approve anything, and does not count as a workspace's administrator. Creating the account, and any grant made while it is unclaimed, is recorded in the audit. Next, a Workspace administrator adds the person to groups.

Disable or reactivate an account

Where: Site configuration, Accounts, the account's page

  1. Open the account. Its page shows what the person holds in every workspace.
  2. Choose Disable account. If they are the last Workspace administrator of any workspace, the confirmation lists those workspaces; the disable still goes ahead.
  3. Confirm.

Their sessions end immediately, a running export stops, and any assistant action waiting for their confirmation is refused. Their group memberships are kept.

To reactivate, open the account and reactivate it. The person gets an emailed link to set a new password and enrol a second factor again. Their memberships return at once, except places in groups with View responses or Erase responses, which stay suspended until a Workspace administrator in each workspace, or a Site administrator, re-confirms them.

Disabling a Site administrator is the same Disable account. You re-enter your password and second factor, and where there are three or more Site administrators, another one, not the person being disabled, must approve it under Approval requests. With exactly two, your password and second factor are enough, and the audit records it. There is no separate suspend.

Disabling an account removes its Site administrator role. Reactivating the account does not give it back.

Accounts cannot be deleted, only disabled, so the audit history keeps its names.

If refused: you cannot disable the last active Site administrator. Workspace administrators cannot disable accounts at all.

Reset someone's sign-in

Where: Site configuration, Accounts, the account's page

  1. Open the account.
  2. Reset its password or its second factor, and confirm.

All of the person's sessions end at the same moment. Their places in groups with View responses or Erase responses are suspended until a Workspace administrator of each workspace, or a Site administrator, re-confirms them; other access keeps working. The reset is recorded in the site audit and in each affected workspace's audit.

By default, where there is an administrator other than you and the person affected, they do the re-confirmation, not you. Where there is none, you may re-confirm, and the audit records that you did.

By default, the person signs in again through an emailed single-use link, then sets a new password after a password reset or enrols a new second factor after a factor reset.

Appoint another Site administrator

Where: Site configuration, Site administrators

  1. Choose the account to promote, or the Site administrator to demote.
  2. Confirm with your password and second factor.
  3. If there are two or more Site administrators, the change waits as an approval request until another Site administrator approves it. The person being demoted never counts as the approver: with exactly two Site administrators, your password and second factor alone demote the other, and the audit records it.

Only Site administrators who are active and have claimed their account count, approve or can be the last one. You cannot promote an account that has not been claimed yet. New accounts are never Site administrators. By default, when you are the only Site administrator, your password and second factor alone are enough to promote a second one, and the last active Site administrator can never be demoted. Every change is recorded in an audit that nobody can edit.

Decide approval requests

Where: Site configuration, Approval requests

Approve or reject a pending request from another Site administrator, or a workspace request to turn second approval off, to let an administrator give themselves response access, or to move a form out of a workspace. You cannot approve your own, including a form move you made yourself. A request that nobody decides expires after 7 days and can no longer be approved. Requests, approvals, rejections and expiries are all recorded in the site audit.

Create a workspace

Where: Site configuration, New workspace

  1. Name the workspace.
  2. Enter the email address of its first Workspace administrator. It defaults to you.
  3. Confirm.

The workspace opens with the four starter groups, empty, and a short checklist. The Workspace administrator then adds people to groups. Only Site administrators can create workspaces.

To make a workspace the shared question library, use Shared question library in Site configuration. It appears when the site has two or more workspaces.

Read the site audit

Where: Site configuration, Site audit

Records account acts (create, disable, reactivate, reset), Site administrator acts (promotions, demotions and approval requests) and marking the shared question library. An act that affects a workspace is also copied into that workspace's audit. Nobody can edit or delete an entry.

Publishing a form

Publish makes a version live in one environment. The same numbered version moves from test to prod, so what you tested is exactly what goes live.

Publish a draft to test

Who: Edit forms and Publish forms for test (Authors has both) · Where: Forms, the form's Publish screen

  1. Open the form and go to Publish.
  2. Choose Publish to test.

The draft is frozen as the next numbered version and goes live in test in one step. Edit forms alone cannot freeze a version.

Publish the same version to prod

Who: Publish forms for prod (Publishers) · Where: Forms, the form's Publish screen

  1. Open the form and go to Publish.
  2. Pick the version already published to test.
  3. Choose Publish to prod.

Nothing is frozen or changed; the same version becomes live in prod. prod only accepts a version already published to another environment, so a draft never goes straight to prod. To roll back, publish an earlier version.

On a site whose only environment is prod, there is nowhere else to publish first, so a draft can be published straight to prod. This needs Edit forms and Publish forms for prod.

With second approval on

If you froze the version, you cannot publish it to prod yourself. Another person with Publish forms for prod publishes it from the form and is recorded as the approver. There is no separate approvals list for this.

On a site whose only environment is prod, publishing a draft to prod creates a request instead. Another person with Publish forms for prod approves it on the form; the draft is then frozen as the next version and goes live in one step. A request lasts 7 days.

Run a live form

Who: Publish forms for that environment · Where: the form's Availability, links and webhooks screens

Open or close the form, create or revoke links, and set up webhooks. In prod with second approval on, a webhook change waits until another holder of Publish forms for prod confirms it.

Read or erase responses

View and export responses with View responses for that environment. Erase responses with Erase responses for that environment; you confirm by typing, and you cannot read what you erase. Both are recorded in the workspace audit.

A one-person site

When QCMS is first set up, the first account is the Site administrator, the Workspace administrator of the first workspace, and a member of all four starter groups. Second approval is off. One person can build, publish and read responses straight away.

  • Second approval cannot be turned on until someone else could approve.
  • Where nobody but you could re-confirm response access after a reset you did, you re-confirm it yourself, and the audit records it.

Glossary

Account
One person's sign-in identity on the site.
Unclaimed account
An account whose emailed sign-in link has not been used yet.
Site administrator
Administers the whole site: accounts, workspaces, Site administrators and site settings. Reads no data because of the role.
Workspace administrator
Administers one workspace: its people, access groups, settings, audit and archive. Reads no data because of the role.
Workspace
The only access zone. Owns forms and questions.
Environment
A named data plane, such as test or prod, with its own live versions and responses.
Access group
A set of roles plus one set of environments, in one workspace, with the people in it.
People in this group
The accounts that are members of an access group.
View forms
Role: read forms, questions, versions and publish history.
Edit forms
Role: draft and edit forms and questions, and preview them.
Publish forms
Role: publish to an environment, open and close forms, links, webhooks.
View responses
Role: view and export responses.
Erase responses
Role: erase responses without reading them.
Publish
Make a version live in an environment, freezing the draft first when you publish a draft.
Version
A frozen, numbered form. The same version moves from test to prod.
Second approval
A workspace setting that requires a second person for named acts in prod.
Approver
The second person under second approval.
Grant, Revoke
Add a person to, or remove them from, an access group.
Authors
Starter group: Edit forms, Publish forms, View responses; every environment but prod. Edit forms only on a site whose only environment is prod.
Publishers
Starter group: Publish forms; prod.
Response viewers
Starter group: View responses; prod.
Response erasers
Starter group: Erase responses; prod.
