---
title: Glossary
description: The words QCMS uses on screen, for the people who build forms, administer workspaces and review responses.
---

Every term below is a word QCMS shows you: on a screen, in a confirmation, or in the assistant's
reply. Each one is defined in the sense the product uses it, with a note where a second word means
the same thing or where two similar words mean different things.

This page is for the people who use QCMS. It names nothing you would only meet in the code.

## Access group

A named set of [roles](#role) that applies in named [environments](#environment), inside one
[workspace](#workspace). You give somebody access by putting them in a group, never by giving them a
role directly, and somebody in two groups holds everything both groups carry.

A group only ever reaches its own workspace. See also [Grant](#grant), [Starter group](#starter-group).

## Account

One person's sign-in to this QCMS site. An account holds nothing until somebody puts it in an
[access group](#access-group) or appoints it an administrator.

Accounts are created by a [Site administrator](#site-administrator) and are never deleted; an account
that should stop working is disabled instead, so the [audit](#audit) keeps its name.

## Answer

What a [respondent](#respondent) puts into one [question](#question). Answers are kept as a record of
every value in turn rather than one value overwritten, which is why a reviewed
[response](#response) can show both what was finally submitted and what was changed along the way.

The response screen calls the final set **Locked answers** and the history the **Answer ledger**.

## Appearance

How one form looks to respondents: its colours, its typeface, its corners and its logo. Appearance
belongs to the form's [draft](#draft), so changing it is an edit like any other, and
[publishing](#publish) freezes it into the [version](#version).

Not to be confused with the **Appearance** menu in your own top bar, which sets light, dark and high
contrast for you alone and changes nothing anybody else sees. See also [Theme](#theme).

## Approval request

A change that is held, and has not happened, until a second person approves it. Requests expire after
seven days and are listed under **Requests** on the workspace's [Audit](#audit) screen.

A held [publish](#publish) is the exception: it is approved on the form itself. See
[Second approval](#second-approval).

## Approver

The second person who approves an act held under [second approval](#second-approval). Never the
person who asked: approver and requester are always different people.

## Archive

Closing a [workspace](#workspace) for good. A workspace can only be archived once it owns no forms,
it cannot be the [shared question library](#shared-question-library), and there is no way to
un-archive it.

## Assistant

The helper that works beside you on an admin screen. It reads what you can read, proposes what you
could do yourself, and changes nothing until you confirm a [card](#card).

The assistant never publishes and never sees respondent answers, and a change it made to a
[draft](#draft) is yours to undo.

## Audit

The record of what was done. Each [workspace](#workspace) has an **Access audit** of response reads,
exports and erasures, access changes, settings changes and assistant actions; the site has its own
audit of account and administrator acts.

An audit row records the act, never its subject matter: no answer value, no respondent and no
question text.

## Availability

Whether a form is taking new responses in one [environment](#environment): **Open** or **Closed**. A
closed form tells anyone who opens its link that it is not accepting responses; people already part
way through finish normally.

A form that has never been [published](#publish) to that environment shows **Not live** instead.

## Card

What the [assistant](#assistant) hands you instead of acting: a list of every step and every value,
with **Confirm** and **Cancel**. Nothing on a card has happened until you confirm it, the act then
runs as you, and a card you leave alone expires.

A card's state is shown in words: Proposed, Running, Done, Pending approval, Cancelled, Expired.

## Condition

The **When** half of a [rule](#rule): a test over earlier answers, such as a question being answered
yes or a choice being one of a named few. Conditions can be combined and nested, and the rule editor
writes the whole thing back to you as a sentence.

A condition can only read questions a respondent has already reached.

## Constraint

A limit on what counts as an acceptable [answer](#answer): a shortest and longest length, a smallest
and largest number, whole numbers only, an earliest and latest date, a checked format such as an
email address or a postcode, or how many options may be selected.

A constraint that is not met shows the question's [validation message](#validation-message). See
also [Required](#required).

## Deprecated

A [question](#question) version marked as one nobody should put on new forms. Forms already using it
keep working, because a published version never changes.

## Draft

The working copy of a form or a question, the only part you can edit, and the thing nobody filling in
a form ever sees. A form has one draft that everybody with [Edit forms](#edit-forms) shares, which is
why the publish screen calls it **the shared draft**.

[Publishing](#publish) turns a draft into a [version](#version). See also [Freeze](#freeze).

## Edit forms

The [role](#role) that lets somebody build: draft and edit forms and questions, pin shared questions
and preview them. It is the same in every [environment](#environment), and it cannot publish on its
own.

## Environment

A separate place for real respondent data, with its own responses, its own links and its own open or
closed state, over the same forms and questions. Most sites run a test environment and a production
one; the names are chosen per site.

A [version](#version) is **live** in an environment once it has been published there, and the same
version can be live in two environments at once. The environment chooser shows whatever name your
site gave each one.

## Erase

Permanently removing one respondent's data. What is erased cannot be recovered, a record that the
session existed and was erased stays behind, and anything already sent to a
[webhook](#webhook) is unaffected.

You confirm an erasure by typing the session's own identifier. See also
[Erase responses](#erase-responses).

## Erase responses

The [role](#role) that lets somebody erase responses and clear their flags in the group's
[environments](#environment), without being able to read any of them.

## Expiry

The day a [secure link](#secure-link) stops working. A link can also stop early by being
[revoked](#revoke), or by being used once if it is a [one-time link](#one-time-link).

## Export

A download of responses, as CSV or JSON, for a chosen [version](#version) and date range. The CSV
comes in a long shape, one row per answer, or a wide shape, one row per response.

Exporting is recorded in the [audit](#audit).

## Flagged

A marking on a [response](#response) that came in looking automated, for instance submitted faster
than the form's minimum time. A flagged response is held back from
[webhook](#webhook) delivery until somebody looks at it and releases the withheld message.

## Form

One questionnaire: ordered [steps](#step) of [questions](#question), plus the [rules](#rule) that
decide what each respondent sees. A form belongs to exactly one [workspace](#workspace).

A form has one editable [draft](#draft) and any number of frozen [versions](#version).

## Freeze

What [publishing](#publish) does to a [draft](#draft): fixes it as the next numbered
[version](#version), which never changes afterwards. The version screens record who froze a version
and, where there was one, who approved it.

## Grant

Putting somebody into an [access group](#access-group), which is how they get access. A
[Workspace administrator](#workspace-administrator) grants by exact email address only; there is no
browsing of the people on the site.

The opposite is [Revoke](#revoke).

## Help text

The line under a [question's](#question) [label](#label) that tells a respondent how to answer it.
Optional, and it travels with the question wherever it is used.

## Identifier

The short, fixed name a [question](#question), [form](#form), [option](#option) or
[workspace](#workspace) is known by for life. It is set when the thing is created and never changes,
even when the name people read does, which is what lets an [export](#export) or a
[rule](#rule) keep pointing at the same thing.

Workspace settings shows it as **Stored identifier**, and says it cannot change.

## Instance

One repeat inside a [repeating group](#repeating-group): one vehicle, one child, one previous
address. Each instance gets its own heading and its own set of answers.

## Label

The wording of a [question](#question) as a respondent reads it, and the wording of each
[option](#option). It is the question's own text, so changing it means a new question
[version](#version).

## Live

Published and in use. A [version](#version) is live in an [environment](#environment) once it has
been published there, and the form screens say **Live now**, **Live in** or **Not live** accordingly.

Live is not the same as [Open](#availability): a live form that has been closed is still live, and
simply takes no new responses.

## Member

Somebody who is part of a [workspace](#workspace). The **Members** screen lists them with the
[access groups](#access-group) each one holds and whether they are a
[Workspace administrator](#workspace-administrator).

Inside one group the same people are listed as **People in this group**.

## Move form

Moving a form, and its responses, to another [workspace](#workspace). The destination's
[access groups](#access-group) take over at once, so the confirmation names who gains and who loses
access first.

The form keeps the [appearance](#appearance) it already had. Questions never move between workspaces;
the [shared question library](#shared-question-library) is how a question is used in more than one.

## Not needed

What a respondent sees against a [step](#step) that a [rule](#rule) has decided they do not have to
answer. It means skipped on purpose, not missed.

## One-time link

A [secure link](#secure-link) that stops working after it has been opened once. A link that is not
one-time can be opened again until it expires or is [revoked](#revoke).

## Option

One of the choices offered by a **Choose one** or **Choose several**
[question](#question). Options are ordered, and each one keeps its
[identifier](#identifier) when it is renamed, so a [rule](#rule) that reads it keeps working.

## Pin

Putting a [question](#question) on a [step](#step) at one exact [version](#version). Pinning is what
makes a form's wording stable: a new version of the question does not change the form until somebody
moves the pin.

## Preview

Looking at a [draft](#draft) the way a respondent would, without anything being published and without
any answer being kept. The form builder can also share a preview with somebody who is not editing it.

## Production

See [Environment](#environment). It is the environment that holds real respondent data, the one
[second approval](#second-approval) protects, and the only one a form has to reach a test environment
before.

## Public form link

The plain web address of a form that anyone may fill in. It works while the form is published and
[open](#availability) in that [environment](#environment), and anyone opening it starts their own
response.

Not to be confused with a [secure link](#secure-link), which is issued to one person.

## Publish

Making a [version](#version) live in one [environment](#environment). Publishing a [draft](#draft)
freezes it as the next version and makes that version live, in one act; publishing an existing
version to another environment moves the same version across and freezes nothing.

Where a site has a test environment, production only accepts a version that has already been
published to a non-production one. Going back is the same act: publishing an earlier version makes
it live again, freezing nothing and losing nothing.

## Publish forms

The [role](#role) that lets somebody put versions live in the group's
[environments](#environment), open and close forms there, and change [secure links](#secure-link) and
[webhooks](#webhook) there.

Publishing a draft needs this and [Edit forms](#edit-forms) together.

## Question

One thing you ask: a [label](#label), a [type](#question-type), optional
[help text](#help-text), whether an answer is [required](#required), and any
[constraints](#constraint). A question lives in the [question library](#question-library) and is used
on a form by being [pinned](#pin) to a [step](#step).

A question is versioned in its own right, so the wording a form shows is the wording it was pinned to.

## Question library

The workspace's collection of questions, which is where every question on every form comes from.
Questions are written and published there once and used on as many forms as you like.

See also [Shared question library](#shared-question-library).

## Question type

What kind of answer a [question](#question) takes. There are seven, and the type is fixed when the
question is created:

- **Short answer**, a line of writing
- **Longer answer**, a paragraph or more
- **Number**
- **Date**
- **Yes or no**, whose two labels you can reword
- **Choose one**, a single [option](#option) from a list
- **Choose several**, any number of options from a list

Each type offers its own [constraints](#constraint).

## Reference

The code a respondent is shown on the thank-you page after they submit, so they have something to
quote if they get in touch.

## Repeating group

A part of a form that a respondent fills in more than once: one set of [questions](#question) asked
per vehicle, per child, per previous address. Each repeat is an [instance](#instance).

How many instances there are is either fixed, taken from an earlier answer, or up to the respondent
between a minimum and a maximum. How they are laid out is your choice of all on one page, one page
per instance, or a table. A repeating group cannot contain another repeating group.

## Required

A [question](#question) a respondent has to answer before they can continue. A question is only
required when it is actually shown: a question a [rule](#rule) has hidden is never required.

The editor's wording is **An answer is required**.

## Respondent

Somebody filling in a form. Respondents are not accounts, they do not sign in, and QCMS keeps no name
for them beyond whatever a form itself asks.

## Response

Everything one respondent gave to one form: their [answers](#answer), when they submitted, and which
[version](#version) they answered. The Responses screen lists them per
[environment](#environment).

The record is also called a **session**, which is the word used before it is submitted. See
[Session](#session), [Submit](#submit).

## Revoke

Taking something back. Revoking a [secure link](#secure-link) stops it working immediately, with no
effect on anybody already part way through. Revoking somebody from an
[access group](#access-group) takes effect on their very next action.

The opposite of revoking access is [Grant](#grant).

## Role

One named thing an [account](#account) is allowed to do, carried by an
[access group](#access-group) rather than given out on its own. There are five:
[View forms](#view-forms), [Edit forms](#edit-forms), [Publish forms](#publish-forms),
[View responses](#view-responses) and [Erase responses](#erase-responses).

View forms and Edit forms apply everywhere; the other three apply only in the group's
[environments](#environment). Administering a workspace is not a role: see
[Workspace administrator](#workspace-administrator).

## Rule

A sentence of the form _when this, show that_: a [condition](#condition) over earlier answers, and
the [questions](#question) or [steps](#step) it reveals. Everything a rule can show is hidden until
its condition is met.

A rule can only show something that comes after every question its condition reads, which is what
makes a form's path predictable.

## Second approval

A [workspace](#workspace) setting, off by default, that requires a second person for the acts that
matter most in production: publishing a version, and changing a
[webhook](#webhook) endpoint. It also means nobody can give themselves access to responses.

The second person is the [Approver](#approver), and is never the person who asked. Turning the
setting off needs an approver too. See [Approval request](#approval-request).

## Secure link

A link issued to one person so they, and only they, can open one form in one
[environment](#environment). It can be [one-time](#one-time-link), it has an
[expiry](#expiry), and it can be [revoked](#revoke).

You make them with **Create** on the Links screen. The web addresses are shown once, then, and cannot
be shown again, so copy or download them before you leave. A secure link for a test
[environment](#environment) is often called a test link, which is the same thing said shorter.

## Session

One respondent's run through a form, from the moment they open the link until they submit. A session
stays on the [version](#version) it started on, so a publish midway never changes the form under
somebody filling it in, and answers are saved as they go so it can be resumed.

Once submitted, the same record is read as a [response](#response).

## Shared question library

One [workspace](#workspace), chosen for the whole site, whose questions everybody with
[Edit forms](#edit-forms) can see and [pin](#pin) on their own forms. Only editors in that workspace
can change them.

It is how one question is used across workspaces, since questions never move between workspaces.

## Site administrator

Somebody who administers the whole site: [accounts](#account), [workspaces](#workspace), other Site
administrators and site settings. The role reads no form and no [response](#response) by itself; a
Site administrator who needs that joins an [access group](#access-group) like anybody else.

Appointing, removing or disabling a Site administrator needs the administrator's own password and
second factor again, and usually a second Site administrator's approval.

## Starter group

One of the four [access groups](#access-group) every new [workspace](#workspace) is created with:
**Authors** (build and read responses outside production), **Publishers** (publish to production),
**Response viewers** and **Response erasers**.

They are ordinary groups. Rename them, change them or delete them as the workspace needs.

## Step

One page of a form: a title and the [questions](#question) on it. A respondent moves through steps
with Continue and Back, and the step list down the side shows which are done and which are
[not needed](#not-needed).

## Submit

What a respondent does at the end of a form, and the point at which their answers stop being editable
and become a [response](#response). Submitting is what sends the [webhook](#webhook) and what puts
the response on the Responses screen.

## Suspended

What happens to somebody's access to responses after their password or second factor is reset: it
stops granting anything until a [Workspace administrator](#workspace-administrator) re-confirms it,
per workspace. Their other access is unaffected.

Nobody re-confirms their own, and nor, normally, does whoever performed the reset.

## Theme

A saved set of looks a [workspace](#workspace) keeps and applies to forms. Applying one copies its
colours, typeface, corners and logo into the form's [draft](#draft); afterwards the form holds them
itself, so changing the theme later changes no existing form.

A workspace administrator can set an **appearance lock**, which stops a form's
[appearance](#appearance) being changed at all. The named sets you try a form against while
[previewing](#preview) it are palettes, not themes, and nothing you pick there is saved to the form.

## Unclaimed account

An [account](#account) whose emailed sign-in link has not been used yet. It holds nothing, cannot
act, cannot approve anything and does not count as a second administrator until the person has signed
in, set a password and enrolled a second factor.

## Validation message

What a respondent is told when their [answer](#answer) does not pass a
[constraint](#constraint) or a [required](#required) question is empty. Each message has a sensible
default and can be reworded per question.

## Version

A numbered, frozen form or question. A version is fixed the moment it is created and never changes,
which is what lets you say exactly what somebody was asked months later.

Form versions are made by [publishing](#publish) a [draft](#draft) and are made
[live](#live) per [environment](#environment); question versions are [pinned](#pin) onto forms.

## View forms

The [role](#role) that lets somebody read the workspace's questions, forms, publish history, links
and webhook settings, and change nothing. It reads no [responses](#response), and it sees a
[webhook](#webhook) address only in part.

## View responses

The [role](#role) that lets somebody read and [export](#export) responses in the group's
[environments](#environment). Every read and every export is written to the [audit](#audit).

## Webhook

An address QCMS sends each [submission](#submit) to, so another system hears about it without anybody
exporting anything. Each endpoint has a signing secret, shown once when it is created, that the
receiving system uses to check the message really came from QCMS.

Deliveries that keep failing end up in the **Dead-letter queue**, where they can be sent again. In
production, changing an endpoint takes a second person under
[second approval](#second-approval).

## Workspace

The container everything belongs to, and the only place access is decided: its forms, its questions,
its [members](#member) and its [access groups](#access-group). A site can run as many as it needs,
and nothing in one workspace reaches another.

Created by a [Site administrator](#site-administrator), who names its first administrator. See
[Archive](#archive), [Move form](#move-form).

## Workspace administrator

Somebody who administers one [workspace](#workspace): its people, its
[access groups](#access-group), its settings, its [audit](#audit) and its
[archive](#archive). The job reads no form and no [response](#response) by itself; an administrator
who needs that is in an [access group](#access-group) too.

A workspace can never be left without one.
