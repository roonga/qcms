Manage access to your forms

Give people the access they need to create forms, publish them and work with responses. Use one group for full form management, or narrower groups for different responsibilities.

About this guide: this describes the agreed access model. The application does not yet implement all of these changes. The guide describes intended task locations and flows. Interface layout, controls and visual design remain open.

How access works

A workspace contains forms and has its own people and access arrangements. One account can have different responsibilities in different workspaces.

There are two separate kinds of group:

  • Form creators contains people allowed to create forms in the workspace. It has no form list or environment scope.
  • Form access groups give their members permissions on explicitly assigned forms. Applicable permissions each select their own environments.

Creating a form and being allowed to work on it are separate. A creator chooses its initial viewers, editors and publishers, and may include themselves. They receive no automatic access to existing forms or to the new form.

Example

Sam can create a Claims form and assign Priya as its editor. If Sam is not assigned any form access, Sam cannot view or edit it afterward simply because they created it.

Groups never cross workspaces. New forms and environments do not silently expand existing access.

Understand workspace boundaries

Each form and group belongs to exactly one workspace. Permissions in Claims do not carry into Underwriting, even if the same account or group name appears in both.

Workspace administrator status is assigned separately in each workspace. Form creators membership permits creation only in its own workspace. Form access groups may cover only that workspace’s forms.

Accounts and environments belong to the installation. An Installation administrator can administer workspaces, but needs explicit form permissions to edit, publish or read responses.

Example

Priya can be a Workspace administrator in Claims and a response viewer in Underwriting. She can manage Claims access, but can only read the Underwriting responses granted to her. Prod response access in one workspace grants nothing in another.

A workspace is not tied to one environment. A form can run different published versions in test and prod, each with separate responses.

Administrator status does not silently grant content, publishing or response access. Creation-only membership does not permit changing existing forms’ access or modifying other groups’ memberships.

Each workspace always retains at least one active Workspace administrator. The installation retains at least one active Installation administrator. Ordinary removal or demotion requires a successor first.

Emergency suspension is an explicit security exception: it can suspend the last active administrator or form operator immediately. The suspension and affected responsibilities are audited; recovery is through an authorised deployment operator, with no silent replacement grants.

A Workspace administrator adds a person to Form creators to let them create forms. That is the complete creation-membership task: no form or environment assignment is needed.

The workspace’s starter groups begin without members. Form access groups also begin without form/environment assignments. Form creators is protected from deletion.

Custom groups still support teams

Custom form access groups can combine editing, publishing and response permissions for their explicitly assigned forms. For example, Claims authors can edit Claims forms; Claims officers can read production responses.

A full-management form access group may combine those capabilities for the same people. It does not itself grant creation permission. Creating forms still requires Form creators membership.

Viewing and editing

Edit forms → View forms

Editing includes viewing an assigned form. Publish forms also includes viewing. Creation is separate: it no longer inherits either permission.

Form permissions are granted only through access groups, including during initial creation. To include someone, use a group they belong to. Adding or removing users from groups remains a Workspace administrator task; form creation does not silently change memberships or introduce direct-user permissions.

In the form creation flow

Check Form creators membership in the workspace. The creator selects initial form access groups using authorised metadata. Explain their configured permissions and eligible coverage, without exposing unauthorised member details. No direct-user grant or group membership change is created. Required approval holds the whole creation request; approval rechecks current authority and editor coverage before form and assignments commit together.

When granting or changing access

Start from the form, person or form access group. Explain the affected people, forms, roles and environments. Recheck effective coverage and action-specific approvals in the final state. Creation-only membership does not permit editing access on other forms.

When arranging response access

A Workspace administrator arranges form/environment response access through groups before collection starts. Do not silently extend an ordinary creator’s initial delegation authority to response data.

When handing over a responsibility

Identify a replacement who is eligible and has the exact form/action/environment permission. Apply the replacement and removal together, preserving coverage. Removing one source of access may leave other grants effective.

When suspending an account

Show all affected workspace administrator and form responsibilities. Ordinary removals preserve coverage. Emergency suspension is a single audited account-level action; memberships remain recorded while all effective access and sessions are blocked. This exception may include the last administrator and requires authorised operator recovery when normal administration is no longer available.

When publishing or approving

Check every destination and the exact frozen version. A different currently authorised approver is required where enabled. Pending grants, unrelated accounts and permissions in another environment cannot satisfy approval coverage.

Manage permissions by the task you need to complete

Start with the outcome you want, such as giving a colleague access to Claims forms or removing someone's production access. The form, person and access group are three ways to understand and change the same permissions.

Three perspectives on the same access model

A formWho can work on this form? Who can publish it or read its responses?Give a team access, remove a group's access, or understand why a person can perform an action.

These perspectives do not create separate kinds of permission. Assigning a group to a form changes that group's form assignments. Adding a person to a group changes its membership. The result is the same wherever the task begins.

Understand the result before making the change

Each task should explain the people, forms, actions and environments affected, the access that will remain, and whether approval is needed.

Give a team access to a form

Assigning Claims officers to a form gives every member of that group its configured permissions on the form. The effect should be clear: “All 24 Claims officers will receive response access to this form in prod.”

Remove someone's production access

Identify every group that supplies the person's production access and explain which memberships must change. Removing one membership may leave permission through another group, and may also remove access to other forms.

Changing a group's roles or environment selections affects all its members and assigned forms, even when the task begins with one form. A change intended for just one form may need a different group. It must not silently create a form-specific permission override.

The same authority and approval rules apply from every perspective. Editing does not grant access administration. A creator may establish initial view/edit/publish access for a new form only; Workspace administrators manage subsequent assignments and response grants. Coverage checks still apply.

The screen and flow catalogue gives each task an intended home. Success means completing the access change accurately; layout and controls remain open, and users do not need to think in terms of creating, updating or deleting records.

Create a form and assign its initial groups

  1. The Workspace administrator adds the person to Form creators. This grants creation only.
  2. The creator selects the existing form access groups that will receive the new form. Their members receive the groups’ configured permissions. To include themselves, the creator needs membership in a selected group.
  3. Verify at least one eligible editor through the selected groups. Empty groups, unusable accounts and pending grants do not count.
  4. When approval is not required, create the form and its authorised group assignments together.
  5. When initial assignments require independent approval, hold the entire creation request. No usable form or active assignment is created until approval. Recheck current authority and coverage before committing.

There is no direct-user permission path. Group memberships and permissions are not silently changed to complete creation. Ordinary creation authority does not permit managing existing forms’ access or administering people.

Response-management coverage is arranged by an authorised Workspace administrator before collection starts. A creator who is also a Workspace administrator can arrange their own permissions where the approval rules allow.

One person

The owner holds Workspace administrator responsibility and Form creators membership, and explicitly belongs to the form access groups they select. With second approval off, creation and editing can start immediately once the editor check passes.

A creator may choose groups that do not include themselves. After creation they have no automatic right to view or edit that form.

Keep every enabled responsibility covered

A form must not be left with an enabled task that nobody is allowed and able to perform. Check actual effective permissions held by eligible accounts, including all contributing groups.

  • Empty groups, disabled accounts, unusable provisional accounts and pending grants do not count.
  • A person does not have to be logged in at every moment to count as eligible.
  • Coverage is specific to the form, action and environment. A test publisher does not cover prod.
  • Where independent approval is required, a different eligible person must hold the permission for that protected action.

Check responsibilities as the form progresses

Coverage is checked as the form progresses. Drafting can start with an eligible editor; publishing and response-management responsibilities must be covered before collection starts in an environment.

When the workspace is archived

Archiving cancels pending approvals and blocks new publishing and access grants. Authorised historical reads and access revocation remain possible. The prerequisites for archiving a used workspace, handling retained forms/data and in-flight sessions, and the remaining permitted operations still need a decision.

Changing or removing access

Check the final effective state before ordinary membership changes, group changes, account deactivation and revocation. Adding a replacement and removing the previous person in one change should be possible. Competing changes must not each pass a stale check and jointly remove the last eligible person.

Explain the missing responsibility and who can resolve it. Never silently give the creator or Workspace administrator extra access to fill the gap.

Emergency suspension is account-level: an Installation administrator can suspend a compromised account immediately, even if it is the last person covering a form responsibility or the last active administrator. This blocks sign-in and existing sessions across every workspace. Memberships remain recorded but grant no effective access. Record the missing responsibilities and block affected actions until authorised replacements or deployment-operator recovery restore coverage.

Choose environments for publishing and responses

An environment is a place where a published form runs, such as test or production. Each environment keeps its own responses.

Creating, viewing and editing form definitions do not require an environment. Publishing, response viewing/export and response erasure each have their own environment selection.

Example: one group with different environment permissions

Publish formsdev, test

This group can edit its assigned forms and publish them in dev or test. It can read production responses, but that response access does not permit production publishing.

Responses can be erased in any environment, including dev and test. In this example, the group explicitly permits erasure in dev, test and prod for its assigned forms. Other groups can select a different combination.

Publish forms also allows opening/closing the form, creating/revoking respondent links, and configuring webhooks in the environments selected for that permission.

Environment selections name actual environments. An environment added later needs a new explicit access assignment.

Manage users and their access

An account lets a person sign in. Workspace responsibilities and group memberships determine what they can do after signing in. One account can belong to several workspaces with different permissions in each.

Add a person

  1. An Installation administrator creates the person's account. It starts without workspace, form or administrator permissions.
  2. The person changes their provisional password and completes the required authentication setup.
  3. A Workspace administrator or Installation administrator assigns the appropriate workspace groups. Workspace administrator status is a separate, explicit appointment.

The person receives each group's permissions for its assigned forms and environments. They can receive different access in another workspace through separate assignments.

To create forms, add the person to Form creators. To work on a form, explicitly assign the appropriate form permissions. A form access group can combine several responsibilities where the same people do them.

Check access before changing it

Understand which people, forms and environments a change affects. Changing a group's permissions or assigned forms affects all its members.

A person can belong to more than one group. Their permissions add together. Removing one group membership removes the access supplied by that group, but they may retain access through another group.

Example

Alex belongs to Form editors and Claims administrators. Removing Alex from Form editors may not remove editing permission if Claims administrators still grants it.

Remove access or disable an account

To remove part of a person's access, revoke the relevant group membership. This affects that group's permissions in its workspace; it does not disable the account or remove access supplied by other groups or workspaces.

For ordinary offboarding, an Installation administrator disables the account after required succession and coverage checks. A compromised account can instead be suspended immediately in one audited emergency action. An applied suspension blocks sign-in and existing-session access across the installation. Reactivation is also an Installation administrator responsibility; any restored group access should be reviewed.

Before ordinary removal, demotion or disabling of the last active Installation or Workspace administrator, appoint a successor in every affected workspace or installation. Emergency account suspension is the explicit security exception; audit the affected responsibilities and use authorised operator recovery if the last administrator is suspended.

Account and access changes are recorded in the audit history. Where second approval is enabled, its restrictions still apply to increases in a person's own response access.

Passwords and recovery

Users manage their own password and authentication. Emergency suspension of the last administrator uses authorised deployment-operator recovery. The detailed recovery procedure still needs implementation and documentation; no user or group is silently promoted.

Work with second approval

Second approval is off by default, so a one-person organisation can work from setup through publishing and response management.

When a workspace requires second approval, protected changes need a different authorised person. Being an administrator does not bypass that requirement.

  • Publishing: approval is for an exact frozen form version and destination environment. The approver needs Publish forms permission for that form and environment.
  • Webhook changes: approval is for the exact proposed configuration. The approver needs the appropriate Publish forms permission.
  • Increasing your own response access: another authorised person must approve the increase, including changes to a group's forms, roles or environments.
  • Turning off second approval: another authorised administrator must approve.

A changed version or configuration needs a new approval. For each protected action, check a different currently eligible approver for that exact form and environment. There is no permanent rule requiring every draft to have two publishers. If the required approver is unavailable, that protected action stays blocked; drafting may continue with its editor.

The exact environment coverage of the approval policy, approval rules for links and open/close actions, and the detailed operator recovery procedure are still being finalised. Enabling second approval cannot guarantee that every protected task remains a one-person task.

Working alone or in a larger team

A daycare with one owner

The owner is the Installation and Workspace administrator and explicitly joins Form creators. During creation they assign themselves the form permissions they need. They arrange response viewing/export and erasure for each collecting environment under their administrator authority.

With second approval off, one eligible person can cover every enabled action. No second account or silent permission inheritance is needed.

An insurance organisation

Workspace administrators delegate creation through Form creators. An author can create a form and choose its initial editor/publisher without becoming a Workspace administrator or accessing other forms.

Form access groups still support departmental responsibilities across many forms. Response managers and publishers have explicit environment scopes. Coverage and eligible independent approvers are checked before protected work becomes available.

Offboarding and handover review all affected responsibilities across workspaces. Urgent account suspension is immediate and audited, even for a sole operator or administrator; affected work remains blocked until authorised recovery or replacement.

Common questions

Can someone create a form without being a Workspace administrator?

Yes. A Workspace administrator adds them to Form creators. That grants creation only.

Can a Form creators member view or edit existing forms?

Only with a separate form permission. Form creators has no list of forms and does not grant viewing, editing, publishing or response access.

Can the creator choose someone else to edit the new form?

Yes. They select an appropriate form access group with another eligible editor and may omit themselves. The group must have effective editor coverage before creation commits.

Does an empty editor group prevent an orphan form?

No. Count eligible active people with effective permission, not a group name or a configured role.

Can one person do everything?

Yes, when independently assigned all required permissions and second approval is off. Enabled independent approval cannot be satisfied by one person approving themselves.

What is still being decided?

Retained-form archive prerequisites, the approval policy’s remaining environment/action details, and the detailed deployment-operator recovery procedure. Group-only access, lifecycle coverage, whole-request pending creation and emergency account suspension are agreed. Shared questions and moving forms between workspaces remain out of scope.

Scenario test cases and results

The broader scenario matrix exercised 369 configurations with 1, 2, 10, 100 or 1,000 accounts; one or three environments; one or three workspaces; and different form/group arrangements. Additional cases used 1,000 access groups.

4,467 model assertions passed. Of the configurations, 317 covered all declared responsibilities and 52 were correctly blocked by missing independent-publisher coverage. A correctly blocked workflow is not the same as completing the business task.
